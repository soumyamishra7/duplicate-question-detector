How EchoMatch reads a pair

Length-based

Compares how long each question is and how far apart their word counts fall. Near-identical phrasing tends to produce near-identical lengths.

Token-based

Strips common stop-words and measures how many meaningful words the two questions actually share, along with whether they open or close on the same word.

Fuzzy matching

Uses the fuzzywuzzy library's ratio, partial-ratio, token-sort and token-set scores to catch reordered words and minor spelling drift.

TF-IDF similarity

Compares the two questions as weighted word and character vectors, so rarer, more distinctive words count more than common ones when judging overlap.

Learned meaning (Word2Vec)

A word-embedding model trained on the question corpus itself, so two questions can score as similar even when they share almost no exact words — e.g. "lose weight" and "shed pounds".

These 25 signals feed a tuned LightGBM classifier trained on the full Quora Question Pairs training set, reaching {{ metrics.get('Accuracy', '—') }} accuracy and {{ metrics.get('ROC AUC', '—') }} ROC AUC on held-out data. The percentage shown above is calibrated, so it reflects a genuine confidence level rather than a raw score.

The model was trained on English questions only, so EchoMatch flags (but doesn't block) input it detects as non-English. Typo correction is opt-in and shown transparently — it can occasionally "fix" an unfamiliar proper noun it doesn't recognize, so it's off by default.
